THE COST OF GOING DOWN

Your busiest hour is the worst time to be offline.

On Black Friday, a single hour can bring in more revenue than a normal day. When the site slows or fails, that revenue goes to a competitor, and much of it does not come back.

$775kEstimated sales lost by J.Crew during around five hours of Black Friday disruption in 2018.Source: press estimates, Nov 2018
$11mEstimated sales lost by Costco during its 2019 Thanksgiving outage.Source: press estimates, Nov 2019

Lost orders

Every minute offline at peak is revenue that would have been your best of the year.

Wasted ad spend

Paid campaigns keep spending while visitors land on errors or a frozen checkout.

Lost customers

Shoppers who cannot buy from you on Black Friday buy elsewhere, and many do not return.

The recovery lasts longer than the outage: abandoned baskets, support queues and refunds can run for days.

WHY SITES CRASH ON PEAK DAYS

It’s rarely just “too much traffic”.

Most peak-day failures come from a handful of weak points that only show up under load. We look for all six.

01
Search & filters overload the database
Layered navigation and search queries multiply under load — often the first thing to fail.
02
No autoscaling (or it scales too slowly)
Servers sized for a normal Tuesday can’t absorb a 10× spike in minutes.
03
Cache misses & personalisation
Full-page cache bypassed by carts, sessions or promo banners sends every request to PHP.
04
Checkout & payment bottlenecks
Session locking, stock reservation and gateway timeouts cause abandoned baskets.
05
Third-party scripts & integrations
Slow ERP, stock or tag scripts drag the whole site down.
06
Bots, scrapers & card-testing
Automated traffic competes with real customers for capacity.
